# What would cost money, and what it would buy

Nothing below has been bought. The ten films are finished and usable as they
are. This is the decision list, with what each option actually buys, so the
spend can be judged rather than assumed.

Prices are estimates from what comparable generations on this account have
cost, not quotes. Treat them as the right order of magnitude.

---

## 1. The one I would actually argue for — and it is free

The strongest single finding in the research is LinkedIn's own study of
**13,000+ B2B video ads and 550,000+ frames**:

| | Lift |
|---|---|
| An **executive** on screen | **+53%** engagement |
| A subject-matter expert on screen | +40% |
| Emotionally resonant video | **+44%** view-through, **2×** completions |
| Share of B2B video containing **any human emotion** | **7%** |

That last row is the point. Almost nobody in this category puts a person on
screen, and the measured lift for doing it is the largest in the dataset.

**These ten films have no face in them.** That is the biggest gap in the set
and motion graphics cannot close it.

**The fix costs nothing:** have a named, titled person at PCI — the exam
director, a lead assessor, a chartered member — record the five 4:5 scripts on
a phone, and cut the existing graphics around them. The `CAPS` array in each
4:5 film is already a narration script written for the purpose. Same face
across all five builds recognition, and on LinkedIn the same person should
post them from their own profile, which is where the distribution is.

For a body whose entire product is trust, a real person from the institute
beats anything that can be bought.

## 2. Paid options, in the order I would consider them

### A · Narration for the five 4:5 films — **~$70–80**

ElevenLabs, roughly $0.30 per second of finished audio, about 240 seconds
across the five. Retakes add to it.

**Buys:** the 4:5 and 16:9 cuts stop being silent. The captions already match
frame for frame, so nothing needs re-timing.

**Does not buy:** a face. A synthetic voice over motion graphics is closer to
the "content farm" pattern the research warns about than a real person on a
phone camera is. If option 1 is happening, this is redundant — and worse than
redundant, because two different voices across one campaign is a step down.

**My view: skip this if a real person will record. Buy it only if they won't.**

### B · One original music bed — **~$5–10**

A single licensed bed reused across all ten.

**Buys:** a consistent sonic identity, and it solves a real licensing problem
the research surfaced: an Instagram *business* account is confined to the Meta
Sound Collection, so trending commercial audio risks muting, takedown or a
reach limit. LinkedIn has no music library at all. One owned bed works
everywhere.

**Cheapest genuine improvement on this list.** The films are built to work
silent, so this is upside rather than repair.

### C · A synthetic presenter — **~$165 per clip**

**Buys:** a face, without needing anyone at PCI to be filmed.

**My view: don't.** It is the most expensive item here, it is strictly worse
than option 1 for a credentialing body, and a synthetic person fronting an
institute whose product is verified identity is a bad look if it is ever
noticed. If nobody at PCI will go on camera, that is worth solving directly
rather than papering over.
